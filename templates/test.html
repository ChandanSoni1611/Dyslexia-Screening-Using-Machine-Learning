📋 Next Steps and Support Recommendations

Immediate Steps

  • Get a Professional Evaluation: Consult a specialist, such as a psychologist or reading therapist, to confirm the diagnosis and identify specific needs.
  • Establish School Support: Develop an Individualized Education Program (IEP) or 504 plan, which can include one-on-one tutoring, untimed tests, and oral testing.
  • Focus on Multisensory Learning: Use techniques that involve sight, sound, and touch, such as tracing letters in sand, using letter tiles, or tracing words while saying their sounds.

Support Strategies for Reading and Writing

  • Phonics-Based Instruction: Use structured, explicit, and systematic phonics programs to help understand how letters map to sounds.
  • Assistive Technology: Use text-to-speech software, audiobooks, and screen readers to help with comprehension.
  • Writing Assistance: Use speech-to-text software, spellcheckers, and encourage touch-typing to speed up writing and reduce fatigue.
  • Reading Techniques: Have the child read aloud, use a ruler to keep their place, and re-read favorite books to build fluency.

Classroom and Home Accommodations

  • Reduce Workload: Shorten assignments and reduce copying tasks.
  • Alternative Assessments: Allow for oral reports instead of written ones.
  • Environment Setup: Ensure a quiet, low-distraction environment for homework.
  • Structure: Break down instructions into small, manageable steps.

Emotional and General Support

  • Build Confidence: Praise effort rather than just results, and focus on the child's strengths in other areas (e.g., art, sports, science).
  • Raise Awareness: Teach the child about dyslexia, explaining that it is a difference in brain function, not a reflection of their intelligence.
  • Provide Emotional Support: Be patient, as frustration and low self-esteem are common.

Remember: Effective interventions can help people with dyslexia thrive, as the condition does not stand in the way of success. Many successful individuals have dyslexia and have achieved great things with proper support and accommodations.

Continuing to Support Learning

  • Practice Writing: Provide opportunities for creative and structured writing activities.
  • Stay Observant: Monitor progress and watch for any changes in reading or writing abilities over time.
  • Encourage Confidence: Praise efforts and celebrate achievements in literacy development.

When to Seek Further Support

If you notice any of the following in the future, consider consulting an educational specialist:

  • Persistent difficulty with reading or spelling despite practice
  • Avoidance of reading or writing activities
  • Difficulty remembering sequences or following multi-step instructions
  • Ongoing struggles with phonics or word recognition

Note: This is a screening tool only. If you have any concerns about learning or development, please consult with educational professionals or healthcare providers for comprehensive evaluation.
